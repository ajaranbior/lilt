How to use Lilt

  1. Read the phrase. Bold marks stressed syllables, orange marks the main stress, where the tune happens.
  2. A model voice says the phrase first (M replays it, H plays just the melody as a hum). The grey line is the melody to copy.
  3. Press Space and say it. Recording stops on its own when you pause. Messed up? Press Space again to restart.
  4. Your pitch appears as blue dots. The circles along the top show where your stresses landed, and the filled one is your strongest.
  5. Press F to hear your own voice with the fix applied, then P to hear how you actually said it. Copy the fixed version.
  6. Space to retry, Enter for the next phrase.

Reading the screen

  • Grey line: the melody to copy, drawn left to right as the phrase goes on. Higher on the line means higher pitch. Thick segments are stressed syllables; thin ones are weak syllables that should be short and quiet.
  • Orange segment: the main stress, the most important word in the phrase. The tune (fall, rise…) starts here and runs to the end.
  • Blue dots: your actual pitch, scaled to your own voice, so it only needs to match the shape, not the height.
  • Circles along the top: one per syllable Lilt heard. Bigger means that syllable stood out more (louder, longer, more pitch movement). The filled circle is your strongest one. It should sit above the orange segment.
  • Faint bars along the bottom (while recording): your loudness. Each peak is a syllable you stressed, so you can see your rhythm as you speak.
  • Brackets at the right: how far your pitch moved (you) compared with the target (aim). If yours is shorter, exaggerate more.
  • Two rows of boxes (rhythm): the target length of each syllable vs yours. Native English has big boxes for stressed syllables and tiny ones for the rest. If your boxes are all the same size, your rhythm is too even.
  • Scores: each line is one skill (melody, main stress, rhythm…). The bold line is the skill this phrase is training. Read the tip under it.

The four tunes

↘ FallStart high on the main word, drop to the bottom. Sounds finished and sure: “I live in LONdon.”
↗ RiseStart low, glide up to the end. Sounds open and questioning: “Are you coming to the PARty?”
↘↗ Fall-riseDrop, then curl up at the very end. An unspoken “…but”: “Well, the food was GOOD…”
↗↘ Rise-fallSwoop up, then fall steeply. Strong feeling: “That's aMAZing!”

What gets measured: melody (the tune's shape after the main stress), main stress (which word stood out most), word stress, rhythm (stressed syllables long and loud, small words squeezed), pitch range, and clarity (whether Chrome's speech recognition understood the words; this sends audio to the browser vendor).

Tip: use headphones or a quiet room. Exaggerate. Most learners use far too little pitch movement.
